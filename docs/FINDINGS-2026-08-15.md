# Silver, stops, and what the earlier gold numbers got wrong

Session of 14–15 August 2026. Supersedes parts of [STRATEGY-FINDINGS.md](STRATEGY-FINDINGS.md)
— see "Corrections" below before trusting any gold figure in that document.

**Headline: the best configuration found is MCX SILVER100, 2-hour bars,
long-only, 200 qty, stop 50 points, target 250 points.** Over 400 days that
turned Rs 1,50,000 into Rs 5,93,321 (+296%) with a Rs 57,379 worst drawdown —
a return-to-drawdown of 7.73, the highest number produced anywhere in this
investigation.

---

## 1. The label is not the fill

This resolved every disagreement between the backtest and what the chart
appeared to show, and it is the single most important thing in this document.

Pine draws the markers away from the bar so they stay readable:

```pine
plotshape(buy,  location = location.belowbar)
plotshape(sell, location = location.abovebar)
```

So the **Buy label sits at the bar's low and the Sell label at the bar's high**,
while the signal itself fires on the **close**. Reading prices off the labels
therefore flatters every trade at both ends.

Measured on MCX GOLD 5-minute, 13 August 2026:

| | Buy | Sell |
|---|---|---|
| where the label is drawn | 154,468 | 154,700 |
| where you actually fill | **154,700** | **154,500** |

Label-to-label reads +232 points (a win). The real trade is **−200 points**
(a loss). A 432-point gap on one trade.

Across that day's 13 completed trades: label-reading says **+2,454 pts**, actual
closes say **−1,095 pts**. The chart flatters the day by 3,549 points
(Rs 35,490 on GOLDM).

**Rule: to read a real entry, hover the bar and take `C`, never the marker.**

## 2. Data quality — three separate traps

Every one of these produced a wrong answer before it was caught. Any future
backtest must filter for all three.

### 2a. Dead contracts

Angel's scrip master only lists **live** contracts. Pinning today's front month
and reading its history backwards walks straight into the period before that
contract was liquid.

MCX GOLD Oct-2026, daily volume:

| date | volume | bars (of 174) |
|---|---|---|
| Mon 03 Aug | 885 | 141 |
| Tue 04 Aug | **92** | 43 |
| Wed 05 Aug | **138** | 49 |
| Thu 06 Aug | 6,925 | 174 |

The Aug contract expired 5 Aug; Oct only became the front month on the 6th.
Backtesting across that boundary invented a **+4,373 point** trade (Rs 4,37,300)
by buying a stale print at volume 1 and "selling" after the contract woke up.
The 2,629-point overnight "gap" was a rollover, not a gold move.

**Filter: drop any day with total volume < 1,000.**

### 2b. TradingView's `B-ADJ` continuous series

The chart symbol "Gold Futures · MCX" is a **back-adjusted continuous**
contract (flag `B-ADJ`, bottom-right). It splices expiring months together and
shifts older data by the roll spread.

On 3 Aug the offset was ~**+1,930 points**: the chart showed ORB 143,924 /
143,850 where the real Oct contract traded 142,000 / 141,912. Prices before a
roll are **not** prices that could have been traded. Differences *within* one
segment are still valid (a constant shift cancels); absolute levels are not.

### 2c. Holes in Angel's feed

26 March 2026 returns only **7 hourly bars**; the session runs 09:00–23:30 but
the data starts at 17:00. Silver walked 2,347 → 2,254 during the eight missing
hours.

A backtest sees one instantaneous jump and fills a stop 44 points worse than it
should. This alone reported a Rs 18,849 loss where the real loss was ~Rs 10,000.

**11 bars in the 400-day silver series sit behind such holes.** Correcting them
moved the headline result by Rs 8,717 and the worst single trade from
−Rs 18,849 to −Rs 15,295.

**Filter: a genuine session open starts at 09:00. A day whose first bar is
later has missing data — do not treat the jump as a gap.**

## 4. Silver is the better instrument

Same strategy, same 400 days, same Rs 1.5 lakh.

|  | GOLD (1 GOLDM) | SILVER (1 SILVER100) |
|---|---|---|
| contract value | Rs 15,46,750 | **Rs 2,36,272** |
| margin per lot | Rs 92,805 | **Rs 25,990** |
| lots affordable on Rs 1.5L | 1 | **5** |
| cost per round trip | Rs 310 | Rs 91 |
| trades | 67 | 68 |
| win rate | 42% | **47%** |
| profit, 1 lot no stop | +Rs 4,36,149 | +Rs 1,69,265 |
| buy & hold, 1 lot | +Rs 5,72,480 | +Rs 1,26,939 |
| **beat holding?** | **no** | **yes** |

Silver rose 116% over the period and the strategy still beat holding — it
captured more than the underlying move by avoiding drawdowns and re-entering
lower.

### Contract note

`SILVER100` is quoted **per 10 g** (~2,363); `SILVER` (30 kg) is quoted **per
kg** (~236,272). Same price action, 1/100th the quote, 1/29th the contract size.

SILVER100 itself only has ~55 days of history (launched 1 June 2026), so all
400-day work was run on the SILVER 30 kg series. The indicator is
scale-invariant (ATR and HMA scale with price, crossovers are preserved), so
signals are identical — but SILVER100 fills would differ slightly.

**SILVER100 tick = Rs 1 = 100 points of the per-kg series.** Every "point"
figure computed on the per-kg series is 1/100th of a point on a SILVER100
chart. This unit difference caused real confusion; state units explicitly.

## 5. Stop-loss analysis

### Maximum adverse excursion (how far a trade goes against you before closing)

Gold, 2h, 67 trades, points of the gold quote:

| | min | median | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| winners | 272 | 975 | 1,621 | **2,178** |
| losers | 233 | 1,490 | 3,952 | **7,532** |

**No winner ever dipped more than 2,178 points; losers reach 7,532.** That gap
is the only thing a stop can exploit.

Silver, 2h, 68 trades, per-kg points (divide by 100 for SILVER100):

| | min | median | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| winners | 280 | 1,792 | 4,956 | 12,721 |
| losers | 580 | 4,200 | 11,901 | 25,094 |

### What stops actually do

On **gold**, a stop bought a calmer ride and nothing else — profit stayed flat
around Rs 4.3 lakh at any stop above 1,800 points, while drawdown fell from
Rs 1,26,042 to Rs 97,444. Return/drawdown 3.46 → 4.00. **Free, but not more
money.**

On **silver**, a 50-point stop (5,000 per-kg) gave **more profit and less
drawdown**: +Rs 1,97,267 vs +Rs 1,69,265, drawdown Rs 34,875 vs Rs 51,285.

### Two traps in stop selection

**Read the profit column as noise.** Gold's sweep ran +12,588, −6,259, +100,
−12,328 across adjacent levels. Choose a stop from the **MAE distribution**
(stable), never from the P&L sweep (not).

**Slippage kills tight stops.** SILVER100's tick is 1 point, so a 13-point stop
is 13 ticks:

| stop | no slip | 1 tick | 2 ticks | 3 ticks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 pts | +85,356 | +72,363 | **+28,440** | — |
| 50 pts | +1,97,267 | +1,89,839 | +1,82,839 | **+1,75,839** |

The tight stop loses 67% of its profit; the wide one loses 11%.

### Trailing stops are destructive here

| trail | profit | winners killed |
|---|---|---|
| 1,500 | +1,33,139 | **22 of 28** |
| 3,000 | +3,39,421 | 8 |

The UT Bot exit is *already* an ATR trailing stop. A second one fires first and
worse. **Fixed stop from entry: helpful. Trailing stop: destructive.**

## 6. The best configuration found

**SILVER100 · 2-hour · long only · stop 50 points · target 250 points (1:5)**

Tested at 200 qty (2 lots) on Rs 1.5 lakh:

| sizing | end balance | profit | worst DD | return/DD | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **fixed 200 qty** | 5,93,321 | +4,43,321 | **−57,379** | **7.73** | survived |
| 1 lot / Rs 1,00,000 | 6,85,221 | +5,35,221 | −2,10,916 | 2.54 | survived |
| 1 lot / Rs 75,000 | 12,40,940 | +10,90,940 | −5,08,444 | 2.15 | survived |
| 1 lot / Rs 50,000 | 40,93,121 | +39,43,121 | −29,15,432 | 1.35 | survived |
| 1 lot / Rs 40,000 | — | — | — | — | **MARGIN CALL 28 Jan** |

**Fixed size is the recommendation.** Compounding multiplies profit 2.5x and
drawdown 8.9x; return/drawdown collapses from 7.73 to 2.15.

### Why 50 points and not 13

At a 13-point stop, **82% of trades dip more than the stop** — four in five are
ejected before they can work. Win rate 32%. At 50 points the win rate is 49%
and profit is 5x higher.

Counter-intuitively, **only 8 of 68 trades ever reach the 250 target.** Fifty
exit on the indicator's own SELL signal. The target's job is capping the rare
monster, not defining the trade. The gain came from *not being stopped out*,
not from the bigger target.

### Concentration risk — read this before believing any of it

The compounded run (1 lot / Rs 75,000) shows the shape starkly:

```
trades  1–21   Jul–Nov 2025   Rs 1,50,000 -> Rs 2,22,494   grind
trades 22–35   Nov 2025–Feb   Rs 2,22,494 -> Rs 14,11,741  the spike
trades 36–68   Feb–Aug 2026   Rs 14,11,741 -> Rs 12,40,940 net LOSS
```

**The last 33 trades — almost six months — lost Rs 1,70,801.** The account fell
35% from its peak (to Rs 9,20,328 on 5 June) before recovering. Everything
depends on a three-month window; start the same system in March 2026 and you
are down.

## 7. Gap risk is real and exceeds the stop

MCX closes 23:30 and reopens 09:00. Across 261 session opens in the silver
series, **15 (5.7%) gapped more than 50 points down** — i.e. straight through
the stop.

| session open | gap | loss on 200 qty |
|---|---|---|
| Fri 30 Jan 26 | −174 pts | **Rs 34,808** vs Rs 10,000 planned |
| Thu 05 Feb 26 | −125 pts | Rs 24,906 |
| Fri 06 Feb 26 | −124 pts | Rs 24,778 |

**A stop is a trigger, not a price guarantee.** Size for ~3.5x the planned risk
as the realistic worst case: Rs 35,000 on 200 qty, 23% of a Rs 1.5 lakh account
in a single bad open. This is a further argument for 2-hour over shorter
timeframes — fewer session opens held per trade.

## 8. Bug fixed this session

`Journal.trades_today()` used `date(created_at)`. `created_at` is stored as an
IST timestamp *with* its `+05:30` offset, and **SQLite's `date()` normalises to
UTC before truncating** — so `date('2026-08-15T01:16+05:30')` is `'2026-08-14'`.

The counter read **zero between midnight and 05:30 IST**, which would have
reopened the `max_trades_per_day` cap in the small hours. Fixed by comparing
the IST date prefix directly (`substr(created_at,1,10)`). Regression covered by
`tests/test_ops.py::TestJournalOrders::test_trades_today_counts_only_today`.

## 9. Standing caveats

- **68 trades.** Small. Five or six trades carry every result here.
- **One regime.** Silver +116%, gold +59% across the same 400 days. Nothing
  here has been tested through a sustained downtrend.
- **Back-month data.** The 400-day gold series is the Oct-2026 contract, which
  was a back month for most of that history — liquid, but not the front month.
- **The sizing table is the least trustworthy thing here.** Gold's
  "1 lot per Rs 250k" rule made *less* than plain 1 lot because it scaled up
  right before a losing streak. Trade sequencing dominates.
