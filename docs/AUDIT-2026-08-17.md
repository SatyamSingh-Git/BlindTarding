# What running it found

Paper trading went live on 17 August 2026. Everything below was found by
starting the bot and watching it, or by writing tests designed to fail. None of
it was caught by the 563 tests that were passing beforehand, because every one
of these lived in the **wiring between components** rather than inside one.

**Twenty defects. Nine of them would have produced wrong numbers rather than an
error** — the dangerous kind, because the system looks healthy while it lies.

---

## The three that mattered most

### 1. MCX bars were anchored to the NSE open

`CandleSeries` and `fetch_candles` both defaulted to NSE's 09:15. Nothing passed
anything else, so gold and silver 2-hour bars were built on **07:15 / 09:15 /
11:15** boundaries. MCX opens at 09:00, so the first "bar" of each day began 105
minutes before the market and held 15 minutes of trade.

Every backtest that chose these plans passed `MCX_SESSION_START` explicitly. The
bot did not. **It was trading a different series than the one that was
validated**, and bar boundaries decide where a close falls, which is what the UT
stop is computed from.

Fixed by deriving the anchor from the instrument's exchange segment. After the
fix the live signals match the documented study exactly — `31 Jul 13:00 SELL`,
`04 Aug 09:00 BUY`, `13 Aug 11:00 SELL`.

### 2. The staleness guard blocked every signal these plans will ever make

A bar carries its **open** time. The guard measured age from that, so a signal
looked exactly one timeframe stale the instant it fired: 300s at 5 minutes
against a 120s limit, and **7200s at 2 hours**.

The dashboard recorded it plainly at 11:00: `signal_staleness — bar 09:00 is
7200s old, limit 120s`. The bot would have sat there all day, healthy-looking,
never trading. Age is now measured from the bar's close, which is what the
guard's own docstring always claimed.

### 3. Gold P&L was overstated tenfold

GOLDM is a **100 gram contract quoted per 10 grams**. So 100 quantity at 153,327
is Rs 15.3 lakh of gold, not Rs 15.3 crore, and a one-point move is worth
**Rs 10, not Rs 100**.

Taking `qty x price` literally meant:

* the paper broker reported **funds of minus Rs 1.5 crore** on a Rs 3 lakh
  account (it also charged full notional for a margin product);
* the dashboard headline read **Rs 88,900** on positions worth about Rs 9,000,
  while the positions panel showed them flat;
* the fat-finger cap measured a legitimate lot at Rs 15.3 crore.

`Instrument.price_multiplier` now carries it, and the paper broker, the position
book's mark-to-market, and both order-stage guards go through it. SILVER100
quotes 1:1 and was already correct.

## Everything else

### Would have produced wrong numbers silently

| # | Defect |
|---|---|
| 4 | `trades_today()` used SQLite's `date()` on an IST timestamp carrying its `+05:30` offset. SQLite normalises to UTC first, so the counter read **zero between midnight and 05:30 IST**, reopening the daily trade cap in the small hours. |
| 5 | The position book's `mark()` missed the multiplier even after the broker was fixed — and `mark()` is what feeds `daily_pnl` and the headline. |
| 6 | The fat-finger and funds guards **skipped themselves entirely** when no reference price was available. At startup that is every time, and startup is when adoption opens a position. |
| 7 | The funds check demanded the **full contract value** for a futures order, a product bought with margin. |
| 8 | An option plan's reference price fell back to the *signal's* price — NIFTY at 24,287 for an option trading near 100, overstating exposure fifteenfold. |
| 9 | Option strikes are resolved at signal time and **never subscribed**, so their price is never in the tick feed. Orders were sized against no price at all. |

### Would have stopped it working

| # | Defect |
|---|---|
| 10 | `_build_context` constructed `DhanClient` directly and ignored `BROKER=angel`. **`run` and `status` have never worked on Angel.** |
| 11 | The CLI passed `token_provider=` to `TradingEngine`, which does not accept it — `TypeError` before the engine could start. |
| 12 | `risk.yaml` carried NSE's 09:15–15:30. MCX trades to 23:30; every evening signal would have been blocked at the market-hours guard. |
| 13 | Angel answers a breached rate limit with **403 and an HTML body**, and the client retried only 5xx. An unlucky burst during warmup killed the engine outright. |
| 14 | `sl_pct` / `target_pct` were declared in config and **read nowhere**. There was no stop-loss in the live path at all. |
| 15 | `square_off_time` was mandatory, so a 2-hour plan holding for days was force-flattened every afternoon. |
| 16 | Nothing anywhere checked **contract expiry**. Both plans pin one contract by id and never roll; SILVER100 expires 31 August, at which point the token goes dead and the plan quietly stops. |
| 17 | `_signal_fresh` localised the bar timestamp but not `now`, raising `TypeError` on a naive `now`. **A guard that raises does not block** — it takes the signal path down inside a task where nothing surfaces it. |

### Introduced by the adoption feature, then fixed

| # | Defect |
|---|---|
| 18 | Adoption called `_enter()` directly, **bypassing nine signal-stage guards** including the kill switch and the duplicate check — at startup, the least supervised moment the bot has. |
| 19 | Adoption ran *before* the feed connected, so the feed-freshness guard blocked it every time. Then the staleness guard blocked it for the opposite reason: an adopted signal is deliberately historical. |
| 20 | `Action` is a plain `class Action(str)`, so `Action.BUY` is the string `"BUY"` and `.value` does not exist. The adoption path crashed the engine the first time it fired. |

### Cosmetic but misleading

* The dashboard returned **HTTP 500** on `/api/state` — the endpoint the whole
  page loads from — because three call sites did `.strftime()` on a now-nullable
  `square_off_time`.
* The chart defaulted to `plans[0]`, the first plan in the file rather than the
  first *enabled* one, so a fresh page opened on a disabled plan and drew
  "no data" while the engine ran perfectly.
* The backtest panel's caveat about theta and option premiums is hardcoded, and
  is nonsense on a futures plan.
* Paper positions were in-memory, so a restart came back flat — and the
  duplicate guard then correctly refused to re-adopt, leaving the bot blind to a
  trade it believed it was in.

## Config that was never revisited

The risk limits were written for NIFTY options and inherited unchanged by
commodity futures:

* `max_notional_per_order: 200,000` — one GOLDM lot is Rs 15.3 lakh of exposure.
  Raised to Rs 25 lakh, which still catches a 10x lot-size misread.
* `market_open/close: 09:15–15:30` — NSE hours on an MCX plan.
* New: `assumed_margin_pct` (0.15) and `min_days_to_expiry` (2).

## What this says about the test suite

563 tests passed through all twenty of these. The suite is genuinely strong
where it is aimed — the existing adversarial file hardens frame parsing,
malformed scrip masters and tick aggregation, and none of those areas produced a
single defect here.

It had almost nothing on **the money path**: what an order costs, what a
position is worth, and which guards are awake when the order goes out. That is
where every one of these lived.

`tests/test_adversarial_money.py` now covers exposure and margin per product
type, guards with no reference price, guards that must never raise, bracket
exits including short-side signs and gap-throughs, expiry, adoption as a
non-back-door, and paper state surviving a restart. 612 tests pass.

**The pattern to remember: unit tests verify the parts, and every one of these
bugs was in the wiring.** Only running it end to end found them.
